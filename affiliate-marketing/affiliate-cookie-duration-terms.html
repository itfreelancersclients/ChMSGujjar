Analyzing Affiliate Program Cookie Duration and Terms

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

The fine print of an affiliate program often determines real earnings more than the headline commission rate. This guide covers what to actually check before joining.

Quick summary: Review cookie duration, last-click attribution rules, excluded traffic sources, and payment thresholds carefully before committing significant content effort to any single program.

1. Cookie Duration Directly Affects Real Earnings

A short cookie window only pays for near-immediate purchases, while longer windows capture buyers who research before deciding — this single factor can significantly change actual income.

2. Understand Last-Click Attribution Rules

Why your referral credit can be quietly overwritten

Many programs credit only the last affiliate link clicked before purchase, meaning a competitor's later link can override your original referral even after your content drove initial interest.

3. Check for Excluded Traffic Sources

Some programs restrict commissions from certain traffic types, such as coupon sites or specific paid ad platforms — confirming this upfront avoids commissions being denied unexpectedly.

4. Review Payment Thresholds and Schedules

Programs with high minimum payout thresholds or infrequent payment schedules can tie up earned commissions longer than expected — this matters more for smaller or newer affiliates.

Pro tip: Before committing significant content effort to a new program, read the full terms page once specifically looking for cookie duration and attribution rules, not just the commission rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is last-click attribution in affiliate marketing?

It means the affiliate whose link was clicked last before a purchase typically receives the commission, regardless of who first introduced the customer to the product.

Why does cookie duration matter so much?

Longer cookie windows capture buyers who take time to decide, while short windows only pay for near-immediate purchases after the click.

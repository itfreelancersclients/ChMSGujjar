Disclosure & FTC Compliance for Affiliates

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

Compliance isn't optional in affiliate marketing. This guide covers what proper disclosure actually looks like and why skipping it carries real risk.

Quick summary: Disclose affiliate relationships clearly and near the actual link, not buried in a general disclaimer page, and apply this consistently across every platform you use.

1. Disclosure Must Be Clear and Nearby, Not Buried

A general disclaimer linked somewhere in a site footer typically doesn't satisfy disclosure requirements — the disclosure needs to appear close to the actual affiliate link or recommendation.

2. Different Platforms Have Different Disclosure Norms

Why a single disclosure approach doesn't fit every channel

A blog post, a YouTube video description, and an Instagram caption each have different conventions and requirements for where and how disclosure should appear.

3. Non-Compliance Carries Real Financial Risk

Regulatory bodies in several regions can and do penalize undisclosed affiliate relationships — the risk isn't theoretical, particularly for larger or monetized accounts.

4. Consistency Builds Long-Term Audience Trust

Audiences who consistently see honest disclosure tend to trust future recommendations more, rather than viewing every post with suspicion.

Pro tip: Review every platform you post affiliate content on and confirm disclosure appears clearly on each — requirements and norms genuinely differ by platform.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a general disclaimer page enough for compliance?

Typically not — disclosure generally needs to be clear and located near the actual affiliate link or recommendation itself.

Does disclosure hurt affiliate click-through rates?

Evidence generally suggests transparent disclosure doesn't meaningfully hurt conversions and often builds longer-term audience trust.

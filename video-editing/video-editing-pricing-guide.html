Video Editing Pricing: How to Price Projects Fairly

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

Pricing video editing work fairly protects both the editor and the client. This guide covers common pricing models and how to choose the right one for different project types.

Quick summary: Per-minute pricing suits simple, predictable edits; project-based pricing suits complex work; retainers suit clients needing consistent, ongoing content.

1. Per-Minute Pricing for Predictable Content

Straightforward edits — talking-head videos or simple vlogs — are often priced per finished minute, giving clients a clear, easy-to-estimate cost upfront.

2. Project-Based Pricing for Complex Work

Why flat project rates protect complex edits

Projects involving heavy color grading, motion graphics, or multi-camera syncing often don't scale linearly with runtime, making a flat project rate fairer than per-minute pricing.

3. Retainers for Ongoing Content Needs

Clients needing consistent weekly or monthly video output benefit from a retainer arrangement, which provides predictable income for the editor and priority turnaround for the client.

4. Always Clarify Revision Limits in Pricing

Unlimited revisions built into a fixed price can quietly erode profitability. Setting a clear revision count upfront protects the value of the original quote.

Pro tip: Track actual hours spent on a few projects across different pricing models — this data reveals which pricing structure is genuinely most profitable for your specific editing speed and style.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is per-minute or project-based pricing better?

It depends on complexity — simple content suits per-minute pricing, while complex edits are fairer as a flat project rate.

How many revisions should be included by default?

Two rounds of revisions is a common standard, with additional rounds billed separately.

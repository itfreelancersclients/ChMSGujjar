Hiring a Graphic Designer: What to Look For

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

Hiring the wrong designer can waste both budget and momentum on a project. This guide covers exactly what to evaluate before committing to a graphic design freelancer or agency.

Quick summary: Look for a portfolio matching your specific need, clear communication about process and revisions, and evidence of strategic thinking, not just aesthetic execution.

1. Match Portfolio Style to Your Specific Need

A designer with a beautiful but entirely different style portfolio may not be the right fit — reviewing work genuinely similar to your project's needs matters more than overall polish alone.

2. Look for Strategic Thinking, Not Just Visuals

Why the reasoning behind design choices matters

A designer who can explain why specific design choices were made for past projects demonstrates deeper strategic value than one who can only describe the software used.

3. Confirm Communication and Revision Process Upfront

Understanding how feedback will be given and how many revision rounds are included prevents the common frustration of unclear expectations partway through a project.

4. Request Original Source Files

Ensuring the final deliverables include editable source files, not just flattened images, protects the ability to make future updates without needing to hire the same designer again.

Pro tip: Ask a candidate designer to briefly critique one of your existing materials — their answer reveals both their genuine design judgment and how they'd approach your specific brand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I always request source files from a designer?

Yes — this ensures future flexibility to update materials without depending entirely on the original designer.

How can I tell if a designer thinks strategically, not just aesthetically?

Asking them to explain the reasoning behind past design decisions reveals whether their process goes beyond surface-level visual choices.

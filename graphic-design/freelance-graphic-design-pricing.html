Freelance Graphic Design Pricing: What to Actually Charge

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  7 min read

Pricing is where many freelance designers lose money — not through lack of skill, but through guessing instead of calculating. This guide covers a clear framework for pricing design work confidently.

Quick summary: Price by project scope and value delivered rather than by hour alone, and build a simple tiered package structure to make pricing clear for both designer and client.

1. Shift From Hourly to Project-Based Pricing

Hourly pricing penalizes efficiency — the faster and more skilled a designer becomes, the less they earn per project. Project-based pricing instead rewards skill and speed.

2. Build a Simple Tiered Package Structure

Why tiered pricing simplifies client decisions

Offering a basic, standard, and premium package gives clients a clear framework to choose from, rather than requiring a custom quote negotiation for every project.

3. Account for Revisions in the Original Quote

Unlimited revisions built into a fixed price can quietly consume far more time than originally planned — specifying a clear revision limit protects the value of the quote.

4. Raise Rates as Demand Increases

Consistently being fully booked or turning down projects is a clear signal to raise rates, rather than waiting for a fixed annual schedule to do so.

Pro tip: Track actual time spent on several projects across different pricing structures — this data often reveals which pricing model is genuinely most profitable for your specific design speed and style.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should new designers charge lower rates?

Slightly below average can help build initial reviews, but pricing should never fall below a calculated minimum viable rate.

How many revisions should be included in a design package?

Two to three rounds is a common standard, with additional revisions billed separately beyond that.

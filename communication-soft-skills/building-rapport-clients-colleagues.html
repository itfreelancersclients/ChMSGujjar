Building Rapport With Clients and Colleagues

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

Strong professional relationships don't happen by accident. This guide covers practical ways to build genuine rapport with clients and colleagues over time.

Quick summary: Show genuine interest in the other person beyond the immediate transaction, follow through consistently on small commitments, and remember and reference details from past conversations.

1. Show Genuine Interest Beyond the Transaction

A relationship that only surfaces during business needs tends to feel transactional — genuine, occasional interest in the other person as an individual builds deeper rapport over time.

2. Consistency in Small Commitments Builds Trust

Why following through on minor promises matters more than expected

Reliably following through on small commitments, like sending a promised resource on time, builds trust incrementally in ways that occasional grand gestures often don't.

3. Remember and Reference Past Conversations

Recalling a detail mentioned in a previous conversation — a project update, a personal note — signals genuine attention and makes the other person feel truly heard.

4. Rapport Takes Time and Can't Be Rushed

Attempting to force rapport too quickly, particularly with a new client or colleague, can come across as insincere — genuine rapport tends to build gradually and naturally.

Pro tip: After an important meeting, jot down one personal or professional detail the other person mentioned — referencing it next time shows attention without needing extra effort.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can rapport be built with clients over remote communication alone?

Yes — while in-person interaction can help, consistent, attentive remote communication can still build genuine rapport over time.

Is small talk necessary for building rapport?

It can help ease into a relationship, though genuine follow-through and attention generally matter more for lasting rapport than small talk alone.

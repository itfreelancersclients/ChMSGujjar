Communicating Effectively in Remote and Hybrid Teams

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  7 min read

Remote and hybrid work removes many informal communication cues that in-person teams rely on. This guide covers adapting communication for distributed teams.

Quick summary: Over-communicate context that would otherwise be conveyed informally in person, choose the right channel deliberately for each type of message, and build in intentional informal connection time.

1. Over-Communicate Context Deliberately

Information that would naturally spread through informal in-office conversation needs to be actively and explicitly communicated in remote settings, or it risks being missed entirely.

2. Choose the Right Channel for Each Message

Why not every message belongs in the same channel

A quick clarifying question suits instant chat, while a complex decision often deserves a video call or detailed written document — matching channel to message type improves clarity.

3. Build in Intentional Informal Connection Time

Remote teams miss the casual hallway conversations that build rapport in person — deliberately scheduling informal, non-work-focused time helps replace this missing connection.

4. Set Clear Expectations Around Response Times

Ambiguity about expected response times in asynchronous remote communication creates unnecessary anxiety — clearly agreed norms around response windows reduce this friction.

Pro tip: Review your team's communication channels and confirm everyone agrees on which channel is used for which type of message — misalignment here is a common source of remote friction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is video call communication always better than written for remote teams?

Not universally — the right channel depends on the message type, with complex or sensitive topics often benefiting from video while quick updates suit written channels.

How can remote teams build rapport without in-person interaction?

Intentionally scheduled informal, non-work-focused time can help replace some of the casual connection that happens naturally in an office setting.

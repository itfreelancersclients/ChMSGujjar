Communication Skills for First-Time Managers

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  7 min read

Moving into a management role requires a different communication approach than being an individual contributor. This guide covers the shift new managers often need to make.

Quick summary: Shift from doing the work yourself to clearly delegating and explaining context, hold regular one-on-ones rather than only communicating during problems, and practice giving both direction and space.

1. Shift From Doing to Delegating Clearly

New managers often continue communicating as if still an individual contributor, giving too little context when delegating — clearly explaining the why, not just the what, becomes essential.

2. Regular One-on-Ones Prevent Communication Gaps

Why only communicating during problems creates distance

A manager who only reaches out when something goes wrong creates a relationship associated with problems — regular, low-stakes one-on-ones build a foundation of trust before issues arise.

3. Balance Giving Direction With Giving Space

New managers sometimes over-direct out of anxiety about outcomes, which can undermine a team member's sense of ownership — balancing clear direction with appropriate autonomy matters.

4. Ask for Feedback on Your Own Management Communication

New managers rarely get direct feedback on their communication style from their own team — actively asking for this input accelerates the learning process significantly.

Pro tip: In your first few one-on-ones as a new manager, ask directly how the person prefers to receive feedback and updates — it saves months of guessing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the biggest communication shift for new managers?

Moving from doing the work directly to clearly delegating with sufficient context is one of the most significant and common shifts new managers face.

How often should new managers hold one-on-ones with their team?

Regular, consistent one-on-ones, rather than only communicating during problems, generally build a stronger foundation of trust.

Empathy in the Workplace: Why It's a Practical Skill

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

Empathy is sometimes dismissed as a soft, secondary skill, but it directly affects team collaboration and client relationships. This guide covers building it as a practical, applied skill.

Quick summary: Practice genuinely trying to understand a situation from the other person's perspective before responding, separate empathy from simply agreeing, and recognize that empathy can be developed with deliberate practice.

1. Empathy Means Understanding, Not Necessarily Agreeing

Genuinely understanding why a colleague feels frustrated doesn't require agreeing with their conclusion — separating these two allows for empathy even in disagreement.

2. Pause to Consider the Other Person's Perspective

Why a quick assumption often misses the real issue

Reacting to a frustrated message with an immediate assumption about the cause often misses the actual underlying concern — briefly considering their likely perspective first leads to a more accurate response.

3. Empathy Improves Client and Team Outcomes Practically

A team member or client who feels genuinely understood is more likely to engage constructively, even during a disagreement, than one who feels dismissed.

4. Empathy Can Be Practiced Like Any Other Skill

Some people find perspective-taking more naturally intuitive than others, but deliberately practicing it in everyday interactions builds genuine capability over time.

Pro tip: Before responding to a frustrated message, write one sentence describing the situation from the other person's likely perspective — it often changes the tone of your reply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is empathy the same as agreeing with someone?

No — empathy means genuinely understanding another person's perspective, which is entirely possible even while disagreeing with their conclusion.

Can empathy be developed, or is it a fixed personality trait?

It's generally considered a skill that improves with deliberate practice, regardless of natural starting tendency.

Marketing Analytics: Tracking What Actually Matters

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  7 min read

Vanity metrics can make a campaign look successful while hiding poor actual business results. This guide covers how to set up tracking that reveals what's truly driving revenue.

Quick summary: Track conversions and revenue attribution, not just clicks and impressions, and set up proper tracking before launching any campaign, not after.

1. Set Up Conversion Tracking Before Launch

Retroactively trying to understand campaign performance without proper conversion tracking in place from day one leaves critical data gaps that can never be fully recovered.

2. Understand Attribution Beyond Last-Click

Why last-click attribution tells an incomplete story

A customer's first touchpoint often deserves partial credit even if a different channel captured the final click before purchase — multi-touch attribution paints a more accurate picture.

3. Separate Vanity Metrics From Business Metrics

Impressions and reach look impressive in reports but don't necessarily correlate with revenue. Prioritizing metrics tied directly to business outcomes prevents misleading conclusions.

4. Build Simple Dashboards Stakeholders Actually Check

A complex analytics setup that no one regularly reviews provides little value. Simple, clear dashboards focused on key metrics get checked and acted upon far more often.

Pro tip: Before adding a new tracking tool, ask what specific decision the resulting data would change — if the answer is unclear, the tracking likely isn't necessary yet.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the most important marketing metric to track?

This depends on business goals, but revenue-attributed conversions are almost always more important than surface-level engagement metrics.

Is last-click attribution still useful?

It's useful as one data point, but relying on it exclusively can undervalue channels that contribute earlier in the customer journey.

Website Redesign: When and How to Approach It

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  7 min read

Redesigning an existing website carries different considerations than building one from scratch. This guide covers approaching a redesign strategically.

Quick summary: Redesign based on clear evidence of a problem, not just visual boredom, preserve SEO value through careful URL and redirect planning, and test the new design against real user behavior before a full rollout.

1. Redesign Based on Evidence, Not Just Aesthetics

A redesign motivated purely by wanting a fresh look, without addressing an actual measured problem, risks disrupting a site that was otherwise performing adequately.

2. Preserve SEO Value Through Careful Planning

Why URL changes without redirects tank search rankings

Changing URL structures during a redesign without properly implementing redirects can cause a significant, sometimes permanent drop in existing search rankings.

3. Test the New Design Against Real Behavior

Assuming a redesign will perform better without testing it against actual user behavior data risks replacing a working design with an unproven one based on subjective preference alone.

4. Plan a Careful, Monitored Rollout

Launching a complete redesign all at once without monitoring key metrics closely afterward makes it harder to catch and address any unexpected negative impact quickly.

Pro tip: Before committing to a full redesign, review actual analytics data for specific problems — a targeted fix to an identified issue is often less risky than a complete overhaul.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a website be redesigned?

There's no fixed timeline — redesign should be driven by evidence of an actual problem or changing business needs rather than a fixed schedule alone.

Does a website redesign always hurt SEO temporarily?

It doesn't have to, if URL changes and redirects are planned carefully — poor redirect planning is the more common cause of SEO damage during a redesign.

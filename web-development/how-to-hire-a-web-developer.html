How to Hire a Web Developer or Development Agency

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  7 min read

Choosing the wrong web development partner can waste significant time and budget. This guide covers what to evaluate before committing to a developer or agency.

Quick summary: Review a genuine portfolio of comparable past projects rather than general claims, clarify project scope and ownership of the final code, and confirm communication and reporting expectations upfront.

1. Review a Genuine Portfolio of Comparable Work

A developer's general claims of experience mean less than a portfolio of actual past projects genuinely comparable in scope and complexity to what you need built.

2. Clarify Project Scope and Code Ownership

Why vague scope leads to costly surprises later

An unclear project scope, or ambiguity about who owns the final codebase after completion, are common sources of costly disputes and surprises once a project is underway.

3. Confirm Communication Expectations Upfront

Agreeing in advance on how often progress will be reported, and through what channel, prevents the common frustration of long silences during an active development project.

4. Be Realistic About Timeline Estimates

A developer promising an unusually fast timeline for a complex project is a common warning sign — realistic timelines account for testing, revisions, and inevitable unexpected issues.

Pro tip: Ask a prospective developer or agency how they'd handle a mid-project scope change — their answer reveals a lot about how flexible and transparent the working relationship will actually be.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I hire a freelance developer or an agency?

This depends on project complexity and budget — a freelancer may suit a focused, smaller project well, while an agency may better handle a larger, multi-faceted build.

Who typically owns the code after a web development project is completed?

This should be explicitly clarified in the project agreement, since practices vary and assumptions here can lead to disputes later.

Website Maintenance: What Happens After Launch

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

Launching a website isn't the end of the work. This guide covers what ongoing maintenance actually involves and why it matters.

Quick summary: Regularly update software and check for broken links, monitor site speed and uptime over time, and review analytics periodically to catch emerging issues early.

1. Software Updates Don't Stop at Launch

Plugins, themes, and underlying frameworks continue receiving updates after launch — skipping these ongoing updates gradually increases both security risk and compatibility issues.

2. Broken Links Accumulate Over Time

Why a site that launched clean doesn't stay that way

External sites referenced in content can disappear or change URLs over time, quietly accumulating broken links that frustrate visitors and hurt SEO if left unchecked.

3. Monitor Speed and Uptime Continuously

A site that loaded quickly at launch can slow down over time as content, plugins, and traffic grow — ongoing monitoring catches this gradual degradation before it becomes severe.

4. Review Analytics for Emerging Issues

A sudden drop in a key metric, like conversion rate or traffic, often signals an underlying issue worth investigating promptly rather than being discovered much later.

Pro tip: Set a recurring quarterly reminder to check your site for broken links, outdated plugins, and any analytics anomalies rather than waiting for a visible problem to force attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often does a website actually need maintenance?

This varies by site complexity, but regular checks — at minimum monthly for updates and quarterly for a broader review — are generally advisable.

What happens if a website is never maintained after launch?

Security vulnerabilities, broken links, and slowly degrading performance tend to accumulate over time without ongoing maintenance.

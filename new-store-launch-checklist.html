New Online Store Launch Checklist: What to Set Up Before Your First Sale

By IT Freelancers Clients | Updated July 2026 | 7 min read

Launching a new online store involves more moving pieces than most first-time sellers expect. This checklist covers the essential operational setup needed before a store is genuinely ready for its first customers.

Quick Summary: Before launch, a store needs working payment processing, accurate product listings, a clear returns policy, and a plan for order fulfillment — missing any of these creates problems on day one.

1. Confirm Payment Processing Works End to End

Testing an actual test purchase through the full checkout flow before launch catches payment gateway issues that would otherwise turn away a store's very first customers.

2. Pre-Launch Setup Checklist

Essential Launch Preparation Tasks

  • Payment gateway testing
  • Product listings with accurate descriptions and images
  • Shipping rates and options configured
  • Returns and refund policy published
  • Customer support contact method set up
  • Tax settings configured correctly
  • Basic analytics tracking installed
  • Order confirmation emails tested

3. Preparing for the First Wave of Orders

Having a clear fulfillment process ready before the first order arrives — not figured out reactively afterward — prevents a chaotic, error-prone start to the business.

4. Setting Up Basic Tracking From Day One

Installing analytics and conversion tracking before launch, rather than adding it later, ensures no early data is lost during the crucial first weeks of the store's operation.

Pro Tip: Place a real test order through your own store before launch, using an actual payment method — this single test often reveals checkout issues that would otherwise go unnoticed until a real customer encounters them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the most commonly overlooked step before launching a store?

Testing the full payment and checkout flow personally is frequently skipped, yet it's one of the most important pre-launch checks.

Should analytics be set up before or after launch?

Before launch — this ensures early customer behavior data isn't lost during the critical initial period.

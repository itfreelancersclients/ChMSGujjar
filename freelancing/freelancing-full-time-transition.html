Freelancing Full-Time: Making the Leap From a Job

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  7 min read

Transitioning from stable employment to full-time freelancing is a significant decision that benefits from careful planning. This guide covers how to make the leap responsibly.

Quick summary: Build a financial buffer and a base of consistent freelance income while still employed, rather than leaving a job before freelance income is proven sustainable.

1. Build a Financial Buffer Before Leaving

Having several months of living expenses saved provides crucial breathing room during the inevitable income fluctuation that comes with transitioning to full-time freelancing.

2. Build Freelance Income While Still Employed

Why transitioning gradually reduces risk significantly

Taking on freelance projects alongside a current job, even in a limited capacity, validates real demand and income potential before fully depending on it.

3. Understand the Full Cost Shift, Not Just Lost Salary

Beyond replacing a salary, freelancers must also account for previously employer-covered costs like health insurance and retirement contributions when calculating true required income.

4. Set a Clear Decision Point, Not Just a Feeling

Defining a specific income threshold or client base size as the signal to transition fully removes ambiguity and emotional second-guessing from the actual decision moment.

Pro tip: Calculate your true "freelance income needed" number by including all previously employer-covered costs, not just your prior salary — this often reveals a higher target than initially assumed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much savings should someone have before quitting a job to freelance full-time?

Several months of living expenses is a commonly recommended buffer, though the ideal amount varies by personal circumstances and risk tolerance.

Should freelancing be tested part-time before going full-time?

Yes, generally — building some proven freelance income while still employed significantly reduces the risk of the full transition.

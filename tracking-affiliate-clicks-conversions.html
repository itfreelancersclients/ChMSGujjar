Tracking Clicks and Conversions Properly

By IT Freelancers Clients  |  Updated July 2026  |  6 min read

Without proper tracking, it's impossible to know which content actually drives affiliate income. This guide covers setting up reliable tracking from the start.

Quick summary: Use dedicated link tracking tools instead of raw affiliate links, tag campaigns consistently, and regularly audit for broken or expired tracking links.

1. Raw Affiliate Links Make Analysis Nearly Impossible

Pasting raw affiliate URLs directly into content makes it difficult to know which specific page or post actually generated a given click or sale later on.

2. Use a Link Management Tool for Real Visibility

Why cloaked, tagged links matter for long-term analysis

Dedicated link tracking tools let you tag each link by content piece and channel, revealing exactly which specific content drives clicks versus which merely gets traffic.

3. Tag Campaigns Consistently From Day One

Inconsistent naming conventions for tracking tags make historical data difficult to compare later — establishing a clear tagging system early saves significant analysis headaches.

4. Audit for Broken Links on a Regular Schedule

Affiliate programs occasionally change link structures or discontinue products — periodically checking for broken links prevents silent, unnoticed income loss.

Pro tip: Set a recurring monthly reminder to check your top 10 highest-traffic pages specifically for broken or outdated affiliate links.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a link tracking tool necessary for small affiliate sites?

Even for smaller sites, tracking tools provide visibility that raw links simply can't offer once you're running more than a handful of pages.

How often should affiliate links be audited?

A monthly check on your highest-traffic pages is a reasonable baseline to catch broken or outdated links early.
